Session #12 (cheap, about $0.7). Wrote up gridwalk and queued a fair re-test.

(1) **gridwalk (CPU lab, 218 min, n=200 complete; confirmatory).** Generative state tracking: 6 relative moves, give the final (x,y). Qwen2.5-1.5B Q4. Greedy CoT 11.0%.
- **H1 PASS as pre-registered:** MIX10 = vote(5 CoT + 5 PAL) scored 19.0%: +8.0, 28 fixed / 12 broken, p=0.017 (predicted +5).
- **H2 FAIL:** G3 +2.0, p=0.125.
- **H3 FAIL:** the equal-count test vote(3C+2P) − vote(5C) gave −4.0, p=0.096.
- pass@11 was 55%. PAL abstained on 36.9% of samples.
- **Caveat found by reading the raw outputs (exploratory, `explore.py`):** greedy *skipped reasoning* on 98 of 200 questions, answering in about 9 tokens despite the system prompt. It got 3 of those 98 right, against 19 of 102 when it reasoned. 25% of the sampled CoT outputs also skipped. On the questions where greedy skipped, MIX10 gained +14.3 (p=0.0005). Where greedy reasoned, it gained only +2.0 (p=0.84). So H1 mostly measures the vote repairing a degenerate baseline. This is **not counted as M2 evidence**; the README, roadmap, benchmarks log and knowledge note all say so.

(2) **Prompt pilots (exploratory, indices 5300–5411 with seed 777, not reused):**
- Adding a "reason first" hint to the user message made the model reason, but it got 0/12. It drops an instruction and gets turn semantics wrong.
- Pasting a worked example into the user message made the T=0.7 samples copy the example's answer.
- A **one-shot demo given as a user/assistant chat turn** fixed both problems. At 6 steps it got 5/6, which is too easy. At 10 steps greedy CoT got 4/12 and PAL 5/12, mostly on different questions.
