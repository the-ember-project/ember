# Weekly review: 2026-11-22

## Could change a result
- **math-mix7 / math-sc7: "on MATH the number of voters matters"**: these two experiments were run on CPU with a 1,024-token output cap. Their voting gains (SC7 − greedy +11.6, SC7 − SC4 +7.2) may come partly from extra samples that happen to finish within the cap, not from voting itself. The agent's own math-verify run at 2,048 tokens found SC8 − greedy of only +2.8. Item slice, quantisation (Q4 vs fp16) and voter count all differ, so the two numbers can't be compared directly. The cap was never measured as a cause. Both the "voters matter" claim and the session-35 note that "part of the earlier voting gain was rescuing truncated answers" rest on comparing different experiments. **What would settle it:** math-verify already has 8 samples per item at 2,048 tokens. Computing SC4 vs SC8, and greedy vs SC8, from those stored samples needs no new generation. Also report the truncation rate per sample in math-sc7.
- **gridwalk-fairpal: "the whole gridwalk PAL gain came entirely from the demo supplying a solver"**: the run failed its own validity check (45% executed against a ≥80% threshold). Only one neutral demo was tried, and it scored 2.5%, far *below* chain-of-thought (38%). That shows this neutral demo doesn't teach the format. It doesn't show that a demo which teaches the program format without giving away the simulator would gain nothing. "Entirely" goes beyond the evidence, and dropping gridwalk from M2 rests on that word. **What would settle it:** a second neutral demo that passes the validity check.

## Prior work
- **math-verify (a small ORM-weighted vote lifts 1.5B by +5 on MATH, but stays below 3B greedy)**: Wu et al. ([2408.00724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00724)) already compare majority voting, best-of-n and RM-weighted voting across model sizes at matched compute. They report that weighted voting beats majority voting, and that smaller models can be compute-optimal. Liu et al. ([2502.06703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06703)) show 1B-scale policies beating far larger ones, but they use 7B–72B PRMs and don't charge the verifier's compute. Weighted voting beating plain voting is already known. What looks new: a general-purpose 1.7B ORM, charged in the compute count, on 1.5B/3B instruct models and fresh items; within-question AUROC measured against the self-judge and token confidence on the same samples; and a result that cuts against "small plus search wins", since 3B greedy is cheaper and not worse. The agent's notes don't overclaim here.
- **math-3b-sc (scale beats voting at matched compute on MATH)**: Snell ([2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)) and Wu (above) already describe this pattern, and the agent cites both. It is a useful replication at 1.5B/3B Q4 on CPU, not a new finding.
- **Label-free training gains are format/boxing elicitation (m3-math-stack, and the GRPO RANDOM arm)**: Spurious Rewards ([2506.10947](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947)) is already cited. The COT-majority DPO arm is essentially ScPO, Self-Consistency Preference Optimization ([2411.04109](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04109)), which I didn't see cited anywhere. Two papers bear directly on the pending m3-grpo-main analysis. Lin et al. ([2605.19444](http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19444)) argue that most TTRL gains are sharpening of problems the model can already solve, rather than learning. Gao ([2606.05932](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05932)) argues that TRUE − RANDOM, the agent's H3, conflates self-consistency elicitation with reward design. The agent's own measurement, that the gain disappears on items where BASE already boxed an answer, looks like a new and clean result for a 1.5B *instruct* model. It goes beyond the Math-base-model results in the literature.
